# Code and results review — 2026-09-11

*A point-in-time document, kept as written. Its ten findings and what became of
each are tracked in [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md) §9; do not read the numbers below
as current. The probe script it refers to and the artefacts it produced are in
`archive/original/review_checks.py` and `results/archive/original/`, and they
measure the behaviour as it was before the fixes, which is the point of keeping
them.*


The saved results are reproducible, but the system does not yet meet all the leakage,
deployment, probability-coherence, and evidence requirements in `BUILD_SPEC.md`.
The first priority is to repair those gaps before adding model complexity.

This review applies `BUILD_SPEC.md` §§2–3, 7–9 and the evidence standards in
`AUDIT.md`. It preserves the audit of the original notebook and does not adopt a new model
or claim a predictive improvement. Only this report, an offline reproduction
script, its measured output, and actual pytest evidence were added.

**Findings, in priority order**

1. **[P1] Missing historical events can leak the target outcome into training.**
   `f1pred/features/pace.py:136` rebuilds `event_index` from events present in
   observations, while `f1pred/pipeline.py:62` builds the cutoff index from the
   schedule. Removing one earlier event's results and qualifying compresses only
   the observation numbering. In the probe, removing `2014_01` while retaining
   its schedule slot admitted **38 observations from target race `2025_05`**.
   Changing those target scores changed fitted driver effects by up to 0.8482.
   The intact supplied data has **zero index mismatches**, so this probe does not
   establish that the saved backtests suffered this particular leak.

   Fix: construct one authoritative race-ID/index mapping and carry it into every
   observation table. Validate unique race IDs and joins; reject unmatched events.
   Add an integration test with an ingestion gap and target-outcome corruption.
   This is required by BUILD_SPEC §3's global index and no-target-data contract.

2. **[P1] Live POST_QUALI ignores qualifying/grid information.**
   `f1pred/forecast.py:93` never passes `grid_col` to `forecast_race`, even when
   the caller supplies a grid and requests POST_QUALI or RACE_START. On
   `2026_13`, supplying the real grid produced **identical win probabilities**
   at PRE_WEEKEND and POST_QUALI; the latter still emitted `pole_probability`,
   confirming that qualifying was simulated. The backtest explicitly supplies
   the grid at `f1pred/evaluation/backtest.py:79`, so its 69.2% result does not
   validate the live CLI's POST_QUALI behavior.

   Fix: build a shared stage-specific snapshot for live and historical forecasts,
   pass the known grid, and reject a later-stage request when its required
   snapshot is unavailable. Practice stages likewise need to disclose that they
   currently add no practice observations. Check output parity with identical
   snapshots, settings, and seeds. Required by BUILD_SPEC §§2–3.

3. **[P1] The displayed DNF probability differs from the simulated marginal.**
   `f1pred/sim/race.py:208` multiplies every input hazard by 2.1 in 22% of races,
   without compensating in ordinary races. Away from the clipping threshold,
   the resulting marginal is `p × (0.78 + 0.22 × 2.1) = 1.242p`. A 100,000-race
   probe with input 10% produced **12.4232%** simulated DNFs. The CLI displays
   the input `dnf_probability`, while win/podium/top-10 and finishing positions
   incorporate `dnf_probability_sim`.

   Madrid examples: Antonelli **6.29% displayed vs 7.93% simulated**; Verstappen
   **19.61% vs 24.46%**. On tuning, observed non-classification was 11.17%, input
   prediction 12.05%, and simulated marginal 14.93%. On 2026, the corresponding
   values are 17.56%, 13.85%, and 17.24%, so removing the multiplier is not by
   itself evidence of a universally better forecast.

   Fix: define one public marginal probability contract and fit correlated
   retirement draws that preserve those marginals, or explicitly model and
   expose conditional versus unconditional probabilities. A shared latent shock
   with thresholds/intercepts solved to preserve each marginal is a candidate.
   Revalidate all affected probabilities on tuning. BUILD_SPEC §§1, 6.4 and 8
   require coherent forecasts and measured acceptance.

4. **[P1] Backtest field membership uses information unavailable at the stage.**
   `f1pred/evaluation/backtest.py:35` selects actual starters from the target
   race's final results, excluding withdrawals not necessarily known before FP1.
   At POST_QUALI, line 81 additionally drops every `grid == 0` starter. The probe
   on `2022_05` drops **Stroll and Vettel**, although the simulator already knows
   how to place pit-lane starters at the back. The supplied 2022–2025 data has
   13 such starter rows; the supplied 2026 data has none, so this pit-lane defect
   does not change the saved 2026 sample.

   Live entry construction has the opposite mismatch: `forecast.py:31` copies
   only starters from the previous race. For the 2025 opener this produces five
   extra and five missing driver IDs relative to actual starters, including
   missing Antonelli and Bearman. It also carries previous constructors through
   transfers. Final-result grids can contain changes made after qualifying,
   which are not automatically legal at the POST_QUALI cutoff.

   Fix: use timestamped entry lists and grid snapshots available at each stage,
   retain pit-lane starters, and define the treatment of later DNS events.
   Historical and live forecasts must share this construction. Label existing
   results as conditional on the retrospectively known starter set until that
   is corrected. Required by BUILD_SPEC §§2–3 and 7.

5. **[P1] Timestamp enforcement is not connected to the production path.**
   `assert_observed_before` and `prediction_cutoff` have no callers in the
   forecasting/ingestion path; their usage is confined to tests. Forecast APIs
   take a stage and event index, but no explicit availability cutoff. Moreover,
   `f1pred/leakage.py:101` coerces malformed and missing times to NaT and accepts
   both. The helper's fixed race-relative session offsets cannot verify an
   actual session schedule. `shifted_expanding` also shifts rows rather than
   events: two constructor rows in the same race can see one another. The probe
   returns the first row's value, 10, for its same-event teammate.

   Fix: carry event/session availability timestamps and an explicit cutoff,
   enforce them at the shared pipeline boundary, and fail closed on invalid
   timestamps. Keep source fetch time separate from historical availability
   time; fetching old results today does not itself make those outcomes future
   information. Aggregate to one row per group/event before shifting, or reject
   duplicate group/event keys. Required by BUILD_SPEC §3.

6. **[P1] The probability benchmark does not establish simulator superiority.**
   `f1pred/evaluation/backtest.py:95` assigns NaN win probabilities to every
   baseline. `f1pred/evaluation/metrics.py:101` turns these into uniform
   probabilities. Consequently championship, recent form, and even known grid
   all receive identical log-loss. This is disclosed in README, but the claim
   that the simulator contributes essentially all probability quality is not
   supported by a comparison with an informative probability baseline.

   Fix: retain an explicitly named uniform baseline and add chronological
   score-to-probability baselines for championship/form/latent strength and,
   separately, the qualifying grid. Fit their temperature on earlier calibration
   folds only. Compare the simulator with those models using paired race-level
   log-loss, Brier and calibration measurements. A regularized Plackett–Luce
   ranking model is another suitable experiment: its sequential selection model
   supplies probabilities as well as rankings. Its advantage here remains
   untested. [Primary model description](https://hturner.github.io/PlackettLuce/articles/Overview.html).

7. **[P1] Several validation labels exceed the available evidence.**
   `docs/FINDINGS.md:201` rejects calibration using deterioration on the 2026
   holdout, while the introduction says 2026 was never used to select anything.
   The written rationale therefore conflicts with the stated protocol; no
   immutable experiment history establishes a different selection chronology.
   The Madrid DNF-versus-season-rate comparison at line 154 compares a forecast
   fitted on that same season with its training history, not independent DNF
   validation. It cannot by itself justify a `[V]` label.

   Pooled winner ECE of 0.00515 on tuning is reproducible, but the uniform model
   obtains **ECE exactly zero** under the same bins. Low pooled ECE alone does
   not establish an informative forecast, or establish calibration for podium,
   top-10 and DNF outcomes. Binned calibration estimates also depend on binning
   and sample size. [Calibration evaluation research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaicenavicius19a.html).

   Fix: make model/calibrator selection on chronological inner validation folds,
   freeze those decisions, and evaluate on untouched outer folds. For any future
   changes informed by these 2026 findings, the already inspected 2026 races
   must be treated as development data. A historical holdout result is not
   erased, but those races cannot become unseen again. Nested selection avoids
   reusing model-selection data as unbiased performance evidence.
   [Official explanation of nested evaluation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_nested_cross_validation_iris.html).

   Report race-clustered uncertainty, favourite-probability reliability bins and
   counts, separate outcome calibration, and interval coverage. The inclusive
   P10–P90 interval coverage recomputed here is 81.16% on tuning and 83.15% on
   2026 PRE_WEEKEND; these pooled discrete-outcome checks are descriptive, not
   proof of conditional 80% coverage. Proper scores measure discrimination and
   calibration together and should accompany reliability diagrams.
   [Official calibration guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

8. **[P2] The championship baseline is cumulative GP points, not standings.**
   `f1pred/pipeline.py:96` sums only race-result `points` and ignores the existing
   standings adapter. It does not incorporate sprint points or official tied-
   points ordering; at a season opener everyone scores zero and the evaluator
   breaks the tie alphabetically. This is not the baseline specified in
   BUILD_SPEC §7. Use standings after the preceding round, with an explicit
   neutral prior for a season opener, and regenerate the paired comparisons.
   The effect on the reported advantage over official standings is not measured
   by this review.

9. **[P2] Displayed ranking and evaluated ranking disagree.**
   `f1pred/sim/race.py:250` sorts by win probability and assigns `predicted_rank`,
   while `f1pred/pipeline.py:286` sets the evaluated `score` from expected
   position conditional on classification. Both are meaningful outputs, but
   they describe different decisions. On tuning, the top displayed driver wins
   **46/92 = 50.0%**, versus the reported score-based **47/92 = 51.1%**.

   Fix: name win-probability rank and predicted finishing-order rank separately,
   and make reported Top-1 match the ranking actually presented as the forecast.
   Neither score should silently be substituted for the other.

10. **[P2] Reproducibility and audit documentation need consolidation.**
    `f1pred/evaluation/backtest.py:69` seeds simulation with
    `config.n_simulations` (50000 by default), not `config.SEED` (20260913).
    Live forecasts use the latter seed. `pyproject.toml:13` supplies lower
    bounds, not the pinned environment promised in BUILD_SPEC §9; no lockfile
    was found. The archived manifest still names the retired package. Several experiment
    CSVs exist without a checked-in end-to-end runner for their selection steps.

    The simulator cites missing `results/simulator_calibration.csv`; reliability
    cites missing `results/data_quality.csv`. `RESULTS_SUMMARY.md` still points
    to the removed fabricated status tables identified by AUDIT finding 3. Their
    writers are indeed removed; this review found stale references, not a new
    fabricated test-report writer.

    Fix: thread one explicit seed through live, backtest and ablation paths;
    lock the resolved environment; save configuration, source/data hashes,
    cutoff, entry/grid provenance, command and simulation count with each run.
    Check in experiment runners, archive/version older summaries clearly, and
    generate status claims from actual artifacts. The exact reruns above
    demonstrate reproducibility in the current environment despite these gaps.

**Techniques worth testing after the correctness fixes**

- First compare simple calibrated strength/rank models and Plackett–Luce with
  the simulator. This is a cheap way to determine whether the simulator's
  complexity earns its place under BUILD_SPEC §8; no replacement is accepted
  based on this review alone.
- Normalize qualifying measurements within comparable Q1/Q2/Q3 conditions
  before combining them. `sources/jolpica.py:210` currently uses each driver's
  minimum lap across segments, then compares all drivers against the session
  minimum. Changing weather and track evolution can masquerade as driver pace.
  Segment effects, robust residuals and uncertainty for sparse observations are
  preferable experiments to treating every gap as an equally clean observation.
- Audit the team/driver identification before interpreting driver effects
  causally. Equal ridge penalties select a decomposition when drivers and
  constructors are confounded. In a separate synthetic probe with two equal
  drivers per team, no transfers, and only team-level performance differences,
  fitted shrunk driver effects were +0.20894 for both drivers of the fast team
  and -0.20894 for both drivers of the slow team. This does not establish
  independent driver skill. Explicit teammate contrasts, identification
  constraints and hierarchical uncertainty are candidates; the already rejected
  dual-strength/backfitting variants should not be re-enabled without evidence.
- Next prioritize POST_FP2 long-run pace, with stint/compound, traffic, session
  evolution and measurement-quality controls. Treat unobserved fuel load as
  uncertainty rather than an exactly known correction. This is a proposed
  experiment consistent with the roadmap, not a demonstrated improvement.
- Keep the legacy telemetry layer out of accepted forecast features until its
  audit defects are fixed and predictive lift is measured. The prior-corner
  braking selection in `f1pred/legacy/features.py:105`, the warm-up heuristic, and
  the unshrunk wet-skill residual remain in the preserved earlier implementation.
  Thirty synthetic formula tests do not validate those real-data estimators.

For acceptance, use the full pytest suite plus end-to-end snapshot/leakage
regressions, primary-metric and calibration comparisons on the tuning design,
and the paired bootstrap required by BUILD_SPEC §8. Keep rejected families
disabled, and describe any family retained without demonstrated lift as
unproven. Since races share drivers, cars and evolving form, add a chronological
block/season sensitivity analysis to the existing paired-race bootstrap before
making strong significance claims. Improvements, neutral repairs, and previously
inflated metrics must be reported separately; fixing a correctness defect is
not itself evidence of better forecasting.
